The hidden cost of employee attrition

Traditional HR reporting counts resignations after institutional knowledge has already left. Organizations need earlier visibility into the patterns that signal retention risk.

Why organizations lose talent

  • Job dissatisfaction and low role fulfilment
  • Pay below market
  • Limited career growth and promotion stalls
  • Excessive workload and persistent overtime
  • Poor work-life balance and burnout
  • Lack of engagement and recognition
  • Workplace environment and managerial friction

What RetainIQ does

Instead of waiting for exit interviews, RetainIQ surfaces statistical drivers of attrition across pay, overtime, tenure and satisfaction.

  • Audits 1,470 records across 35 HR dimensions
  • Isolates cohorts with elevated turnover
  • Maps job, environment and work-life scores
  • Benchmarks Sales, R&D and HR departments
  • Highlights key risk indicators
  • Lets leaders simulate retention interventions

Validation and export

Cross-checked SQL aggregations against pandas summaries; exported clean tables for Power BI and ML.

Turning findings into action

Compensation: under $3k/month, 28.6% attrition vs 8.9% above $10k

Review pay bands for entry-level roles such as Lab Technicians and Sales Reps.

Workload: overtime 30.5% vs 10.4%

Monitor recurring overtime and rebalance staffing in overloaded teams.

Career development: 0–2 years tenure, 29.8%

Structured onboarding, mentoring and clear 12–24 month growth paths.

Engagement: age 18–25, 35.8%

Rotation programmes and regular pulse surveys for early-career cohorts.

Recognition: low job satisfaction, 22.8% vs 11.3%

Peer and manager recognition tied to visible contributions.

Managerial support: Sales Representatives, 39.8%

Coaching for frontline sales managers and realistic target-setting.

Work-life balance: "bad" rating, 31.3%

Flexible scheduling and protected time off where balance scores are lowest.
